Phone script: website redesign

Plain and friendly. Read it once, then say it your own way. Slow down. Listen more than you talk. Only call business lines during business hours, and if someone asks not to be called again, write it down and don't call them back.

1. Opener

"Hi, this is Dane Dison with Mighty Organix, I'm a web designer here in Middle Tennessee. Is this the owner, or the person who looks after your website?"

2. Ask permission

"I'm not calling to sell you anything today. I was looking at local businesses online and noticed something on your website I thought you'd want to know about. Do you have two minutes, or is there a better time to call back?"

3. The reason (keep it specific and kind)

"When I opened your site on my phone, [the one thing from the list: it didn't load / the phone number wasn't tap-to-call / it took a long while to load / there's no way to book]. Most folks find you on their phone now, so that one thing can quietly cost you calls."

4. Short value pitch

"What I do is build websites that make you money: customers can call, book or pay you in one tap, and it keeps working for you day and night. My motto is precision over speed. I take the time to get it right the first time, and I'm one person from the first call to launch, so you're never passed around."

"If phone calls are a juggle, I can also add an AI receptionist that answers common questions, books appointments and follows up for you, even after hours. It's an add-on, only if you want it."

"And if money's tight, I offer a sliding scale based on what you can afford. I want to help this community grow, period."

5. Close to a free consult

"Would you be open to a free 20-minute chat? I'll show you a quick sample of what your site could look like on a phone, with a set price. No pressure either way. Is [day] morning or afternoon better?"

Common questions and how to answer

"We already have a website."
"That's great, and you've got a head start. I'm not saying start over. I'd just fix the few things that cost you calls, like [the issue]. I'm glad to show you what I mean, free."
"We can't afford it right now."
"I understand, starting and running a business is hard. That's why I have a sliding scale. Let's talk about what fits your budget, even if it's a small first step."
"I'm not good with tech."
"That's who I like working with most. I set it all up, show you how it works at your pace, and I'm a phone call away after."
"My nephew / a friend does our site."
"Good, keep them close. If you ever want a second set of eyes, I'm happy to give you a free list of quick fixes you can hand to them."
"Just email me something."
"Glad to. What's the best email? I'll send a short note with the one or two things I noticed and a link to my work."
"Not interested."
"No problem at all, thanks for your time. Have a good one." (Note it and don't call back.)
"How do I know you're legit?"
"Fair question. You can see my work and prices at my website, and you don't pay anything until you've seen a sample and a set price."
